「学得慢」「孩子不爱练」「练了没进步」——**这三类问题的答案，多数不在技巧层面。** 这一页把它们按"设计问题"重新组织。

## 你在这里想做什么

先判断你遇到的是哪一类：

| 现象 | 真正的性质 | 该去哪 |
|---|---|---|
| 「练了一个月没进步」 | **练习结构问题** | 练习研究 + 刻意练习那两篇 |
| 「孩子不肯练」 | **动机与设计问题** | 教学法三篇 + 找老师那篇 |
| 「我教了多年没长进」 | **反馈结构问题** | 视唱练耳 + 考级那两篇 |

## 去哪读：按问题分

### 一、练习结构：练了没进展

- [练习研究告诉我们什么](../../culture/practice-research/) —— **实证部分：练什么、练多久、怎么练**
- [练琴在改变大脑的哪里](../../culture/practice-and-brain/) —— 长期练习的真实代价与收益
- [每天该练多久](../../start/daily-practice-time/) —— 频率比时长重要
- [刻意练习](../../start/deliberate-practice/) —— 有目标、有反馈、有跳出舒适区的循环
- [慢练](../../start/slow-practice/) —— 慢练练的是"准确"而不是"慢"

**这一组的共同点是：它们都在谈结构，而不是谈"努力"。**

### 二、动机：为什么不肯练

- [「先识谱还是先演奏」的教学辩论](../../culture/notation-teaching/) —— **同一件事的两派主张**
- [三大教学法：达尔克罗兹、奥尔夫、柯达伊](../../culture/three-methods/) —— 三种不同的"以 child 为中心"
- [铃木教学法：像学母语一样学音乐](../../culture/suzuki-method/) —— 母语法的核心是"每天一点点"
- [怎么判断一个好老师（与好学生）](../../culture/finding-a-teacher/) —— 这一篇的判据双向适用
- [成人音乐教育：和儿童班完全不同的设计](../../culture/adult-education/) —— 成人的约束完全不同

### 三、教学法：怎么教别人

- [集体课与一对一：各自适合谁](../../culture/group-vs-private/) —— 决策依据在"目标"不在"水平"
- [考级体系面面观：它们各自在考什么](../../culture/exam-systems/) —— 考级考的是别的东西
- [视唱练耳怎么教：从「听得出」到「写得出」](../../culture/ear-training-teaching/) —— 练耳是可以教的
- [学校音乐课教什么：不止唱歌](../../culture/school-music/) —— 课堂与考级是两件事

## 一个可以立刻做的动作

打开[练习研究](../../culture/practice-research/)那篇，把里面建议的练习结构写下来，然后对照你现在的练法看差在哪。

这一篇的价值不在"告诉你该练什么"，而在"让你看出自己缺了哪一环"。 多数人练了多年没进展，缺的不是努力，是反馈。

## 一个反直觉的提醒

"练琴"和"学音乐"不是同一件事。 站内有几篇专门讲这个差别：

- [背诵与看谱的取舍](../../start/memorization/)
- [视奏](../../start/sight-reading/)
- [绝对音感：天赋、训练与它的边界](../../culture/perfect-pitch-again/) —— 绝对音感好不等于音乐学得好

如果一个学生练了十年琴还是听不懂一段音乐，问题很可能不在技术训练上。

## 还没建好的

四处诚实说明：

1. 没有"教师资源库" —— 有十篇教学法文章，但没有乐谱、教材、作业这类可直接用的东西
2. 没有"如何自己练"的完整方案 —— 讲清了原则，没给日程表
3. 考级那篇只讲体系，不给建议 —— 读完你知道考级考什么，但不知道该不该考
4. 老师那篇的判据偏向"找老师" —— 对已经是老师的读者，缺的是"如何被评价"

第 4 点是本条路线最实际的缺口：教学关系里最难的是双向反馈，而站内只覆盖了其中一个方向。

"I am slow to progress", "my child will not practise", "I have taught for years with no growth" — **for all three, the answer usually does not lie in technique.** They reorganise as design problems.

## What you are trying to do here

First work out which kind of problem you have:

| Symptom | Real nature | Where to go |
|---|---|---|
| "a month of practice with no progress" | **practice structure** | the research pieces plus deliberate practice |
| "my child will not practise" | **motivation and design** | the three method articles plus judging a teacher |
| "I have taught for years with no growth" | **feedback structure** | ear training and the exam-systems pieces |

## Where to read: by problem

### One: practice structure — practising with no progress

- [What practice research tells us](../../culture/practice-research/) — **the evidence: what to practise, how long, how**
- [Practice and the brain](../../culture/practice-and-brain/) — the real costs and benefits of long-term practice
- [How long should I practise each day?](../../start/daily-practice-time/) — frequency beats duration
- [Deliberate practice](../../start/deliberate-practice/) — goals, feedback, and stepping outside comfort
- [Slow practice](../../start/slow-practice/) — slow practice trains accuracy, not slowness

**What this group shares: they are all about structure, not about trying harder.**

### Two: motivation — why nobody wants to practise

- [The notation-versus-playing debate](../../culture/notation-teaching/) — **two positions on one thing**
- [Three methods: Dalcroze, Orff, Kodály](../../culture/three-methods/) — three different senses of "child-centred"
- [Suzuki method: like learning a mother tongue](../../culture/suzuki-method/) — the core of the mother-tongue method is "a little every day"
- [Judging a good teacher (and a good student)](../../culture/finding-a-teacher/) — the criteria work in both directions
- [Adult music education](../../culture/adult-education/) — the constraints are entirely different

### Three: pedagogy — how to teach others

- [Group lessons versus one-to-one: who each suits](../../culture/group-vs-private/) — decided by goal, not by level
- [Exam systems: what each is actually testing](../../culture/exam-systems/) — exams test something else entirely
- [How ear training is taught: from "hearing it" to "writing it"](../../culture/ear-training-teaching/) — ear training can be taught
- [What school music classes teach: not only singing](../../culture/school-music/) — the classroom and the exam are two things

## An action you can take immediately

**Open [practice research](../../culture/practice-research/), write down the practice structure it recommends, then compare it with what you actually do.**

**The value of that piece is not "it tells you what to practise" but "it lets you see which link you are missing". Most people who practise for years without progress do not lack effort; they lack feedback**.

## A counter-intuitive reminder

"Practising an instrument" and "learning music" are not the same thing. Several pieces here draw exactly that line:

- [Memorisation versus reading](../../start/memorization/)
- [Sight-reading](../../start/sight-reading/)
- [Perfect pitch: talent, training and its limits](../../culture/perfect-pitch-again/) — good perfect pitch is not good musicianship

**If a student has played for ten years and still does not understand a passage, the problem is probably not in technical training.**

## What is not built yet

Four honest notes:

1. There is no teacher resource library — ten pedagogy articles exist, but no scores, textbooks or assignments you can use directly
2. There is no complete self-practice programme — the principles are explained, the schedule is not
3. The exams piece describes the systems but gives no advice — you learn what exams test, but not whether you should take one
4. Judging a good teacher is written from the "finding a teacher" side — what is missing is how to be evaluated as a teacher

Point four is the most practical gap on this route: the hardest part of a teaching relationship is two-way feedback, and only one direction is covered here.
