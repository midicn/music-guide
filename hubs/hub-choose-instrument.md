选乐器这件事，大部分建议都在说"哪个好"。**这个问题没有答案。** 有答案的是另一个问题：**你愿意忍受什么。**

## 你在这里想做什么

选乐器只需要三个决定，而且顺序不能颠倒：

1. **你愿意忍受什么**（手型条件、体力、噪音、钱）
2. **你想练多久**（这决定了排除法能砍掉多少选项）
3. 你想要什么样的音乐（这决定风格，不决定乐器）

顺序反了，就会买错。 大多数人是从第3 步开始挑的，然后买回来发现第 1 步过不去。

## 去哪读：三层

### 第一层：先排除（按你的身体条件）

这一层是硬的，不能靠意愿绕过。

- 站内：[预算与第一件乐器](../../start/budget-instrument/) —— 钱的约束怎么算
- 站内：[不被四种说法误导](../../start/uncommon-instruments/) —— 常见推荐里的错误信息
- 站外：[乐器图鉴](https://inst.midicn.com/) —— 每件乐器都有剖面图与音域图，先看有没有你手型做不到的

关于噪音这一条，站内有一篇常被忽略的提醒：[练琴在改变大脑的哪里](../../culture/practice-and-brain/) 里提到，长期练习的设备与环境约束往往比"选哪件"更影响能不能坚持。

### 第二层：按你的时间排除

- 站内：[每天该练多久](../../start/daily-practice-time/) —— 频率比时长重要
- 站内：[先学乐理，还是先练琴](../../start/theory-or-instrument-first/) —— 可以并行，不必排队
- 站内：[电子琴还是钢琴](../../start/piano-vs-keyboard/) —— 一个具体的选择对照

这一层的实际作用是砍选项。 如果你每天只有二十分钟，那些需要每天固定时段练习的乐器会被自然排除。

### 第三层：选风格（不是选乐器）

这一层决定你练什么曲子，而不是买什么乐器。

- 站内：[练习研究告诉我们什么](../../culture/practice-research/) —— 练习结构的实证
- 站内：[练琴在改变大脑的哪里](../../culture/practice-and-brain/) —— 长期练习的真实代价
- 站内：[成人音乐教育](../../culture/adult-education/) —— 成人的设计思路和儿童班完全不同

## 一个可以自己做的动作

打开乐器站的剖面图，随机挑三件乐器，看它们怎么发声。 站内[乐器站](https://inst.midicn.com/)有 140 余件乐器的剖面图与音域图，每一件都能听音色。

如果你看完某一类乐器完全没有想试的念头，那一类就可以排除 —— 不管别人怎么推荐。

## 还没建好的

三处诚实说明：

1. 乐器站按"乐器家族"组织，不按"适合谁"组织 —— 它能回答"这件乐器是什么"，不能回答"我该不该学它"
2. 没有乐器横向对比表 —— 你得自己在 140 多页之间跳
3. 成人与儿童的路径差异只在文化站的两篇里提到 —— 乐器站本身没有面向成人的选乐器指引

第 3 点是真缺口。 站内那篇[成人音乐教育](../../culture/adult-education/)讲了怎么教，但没有系统讲"成人怎么选"。

Most advice about choosing an instrument talks about "which one is best". **That question has no answer.** The one that does is: **what are you willing to endure.**

## What you are trying to do here

Choosing an instrument takes three decisions, and the order matters:

1. **What you are willing to endure** (hand shape, stamina, noise, money)
2. **How long you can practise** (this decides how many options elimination removes)
3. What kind of music you want (this decides style, not instrument)

Reverse the order and you will buy the wrong thing. Most people start at step 3 and discover afterwards that step 1 was not passable.

## Where to read: three layers

### Layer one: eliminate (by your body)

This layer is hard; willpower cannot bypass it.

- Onsite: [budget and a first instrument](../../start/budget-instrument/) — how to work out the money constraint
- Onsite: [four claims to be wary of](../../start/uncommon-instruments/) — errors in common recommendations
- Offsite: [the instrument guide](https://inst.midicn.com/) — every instrument has a cross-section and a range chart; check first whether your hands cannot do it

On noise, an often-overlooked point: [practice and the brain](../../culture/practice-and-brain/) notes that equipment and environment constraints affect long-term practice more than the choice of instrument itself.

### Layer two: eliminate by your available time

- Onsite: [how long should I practise each day?](../../start/daily-practice-time/) — frequency beats duration
- Onsite: [theory first, or instrument first?](../../start/theory-or-instrument-first/) — they can run in parallel
- Onsite: [piano or keyboard?](../../start/piano-vs-keyboard/) — one concrete comparison

The practical role of this layer is elimination. If you have twenty minutes a day, the instruments that demand a fixed daily slot get removed by themselves.

### Layer three: choose a style, not an instrument

This layer decides what you practise, not what you buy.

- Onsite: [what practice research tells us](../../culture/practice-research/) — the evidence on practice structure
- Onsite: [practice and the brain](../../culture/practice-and-brain/) — the real costs of long-term practice
- Onsite: [adult music education](../../culture/adult-education/) — a completely different design from children's classes

## An action you can take yourself

**Open the instrument guide and pick three instruments at random; look at how they produce sound.** The [instrument site](https://inst.midicn.com/) has cross-section diagrams and range charts for over 140 instruments, each with a timbre audition.

**If looking at a whole family leaves you with no urge to try it, that family can be ruled out** — whatever anyone recommends.

## What is not built yet

Three honest notes:

1. The instrument site is organised by instrument family, not by "who it suits" — it answers "what is this instrument", not "should I learn it"
2. There is no side-by-side comparison table — you have to hop across 140-odd pages yourself
3. The adult/child difference appears only in two culture articles — the instrument site itself has no choosing-an-instrument guide for adults

Point three is a genuine gap. The piece on [adult music education](../../culture/adult-education/) explains how to teach adults, but nothing systematically covers how an adult should choose.
