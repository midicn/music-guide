「想听得更懂」通常被理解成"耳朵要变灵"。**更实际的看法是：听得懂等于知道该听什么。** 这一页给你两件事：一份可操作的听辨清单，和十八件能直接点的工具。

## 你在这里想做什么

先分清你想要的是哪一种"懂"：

- **听得出**（这是不是三和弦）—— 需要练耳，站内有一整套工具；
- **听得懂**（它在做什么、为什么这么写）—— 需要概念，多数是文字；
- **记得住**（听过之后还能想起）—— 这其实是记忆问题，不是听力问题。

**三者的训练方式完全不同。** 站内[音乐与记忆](../../culture/music-and-memory/)那篇专门讲第三种。

## 去哪读：按你要的"懂"分

### 一、听得出：先练工具

站内十八件工具都按"一件一个可执行的判断"设计，**建议从这三件开始**：

- [音程是什么：八个距离，一次听清](../../tools/interval-basics/) —— 建立"音程是距离不是高低"
- [大三度和小三度：一字之差](../../tools/interval-quality/) —— 最难的那一对，也是最该练的
- [音程听辨练习：先听得出，再说出名字](../../tools/interval-identification/) —— 卡在哪一层

**十八件的完整清单在[工具区索引](../../tools/)**，分音程、音阶、和弦、进行、节奏、调、音色七组。

### 二、听得懂：文字在通识区

- [音乐怎么引发情绪：期待与解决](../../culture/emotion-mechanism/) —— 情绪的机制不是玄学
- [大调为什么听起来「快乐」：解释与争议](../../culture/major-happy-why/) —— **一个问题的四种解释**
- [音乐与记忆：为什么老歌带回忆](../../culture/music-and-memory/) —— 记忆是怎么被音乐撬动的
- [节奏与身体：为什么我们想跟着动](../../culture/rhythm-and-body/) —— 身体的参与比耳朵早
- [世界音乐这个词的问题](../../culture/world-music-label/) —— **听之前先知道你在听什么框架**

### 三、听得全：先认识结构

**这一层最容易被跳过，但它决定了你能听出什么。**

- [五个音乐作品的标准曲式](https://theo.midicn.com/) —— 先知道曲子的骨架
- [音乐史](https://hist.midicn.com/)与[流派](https://gen.midicn.com/)站 —— 背景知识（**两站仍在骨架状态**）
- 站内：[乐器图鉴](https://inst.midicn.com/) —— 音色是什么、从哪来

## 一个可执行的三步法

如果你只有半小时，不知道从哪开始：

1. **先听一遍完整的作品，不做任何分析**（这一步最常被跳过，但它决定了后面所有分析的乐趣）
2. **查曲式骨架**（站外 theo 的曲式那几章）
3. **带着结构回去重听一遍**，这次只注意一个点（比如"进入的第二乐段是什么调"）

**第 1 步不能省。** 没听过原版就分析结构，你分析的是谱子，不是音乐。

## 还没建好的

四处诚实说明：

1. **没有"聆听曲目表"** —— 站内有很多通识与理论，但**没有一份"按难度排列的曲目清单"**。这是本条路线最大的空缺
2. **音乐史与流派站都在骨架状态** —— 所以"背景知识"这一层实际上只有站外的零散文章可用
3. **工具区没有"听写"（dictation）** —— 十八件都是"听得出"，没有"写下来"这一环
4. **词典站不足以支撑"查术语"** —— 而查术语是这一层最频繁的动作

**第 1 点我认为是真缺口**：它需要一份跨三站的曲目排序，是一份**新内容**而不是新结构。

"I want to understand music better" is usually read as "my ears need to get sharper". **A more useful view: understanding music equals knowing what to listen for.** This page gives you an actionable checklist and eighteen clickable tools.

## What you are trying to do here

First work out which kind of "understand" you want:

- **hearing it** (is this a triad) — ear training; there is a full set of tools on this site;
- **understanding it** (what is it doing, why is it written this way) — concepts, mostly text;
- **remembering it** (still recalling it later) — this is memory, not hearing.

**The three are trained completely differently.** The piece on [music and memory](../../culture/music-and-memory/) covers the third properly.

## Where to read: by the kind of understanding

### One: hearing it — practise with the tools

All eighteen tools are designed around one executable judgement each. **Start with these three:**

- [What an interval is: eight distances at once](../../tools/interval-basics/) — build "an interval is a distance, not a height"
- [Major and minor thirds: one word apart](../../tools/interval-quality/) — the hardest pair, and the one most worth practising
- [Interval identification drill](../../tools/interval-identification/) — which layer you stall at

**The full list of eighteen is in the [tools index](../../tools/)**, grouped as intervals, scales, chords, progressions, rhythm, keys and timbre.

### Two: understanding it — the text is in the culture section

- [How music triggers emotion: expectation and resolution](../../culture/emotion-mechanism/) — mechanism, not mystique
- [Why the major scale sounds happy: explanation and dispute](../../culture/major-happy-why/) — **four explanations of one question**
- [Music and memory: why old songs carry memory](../../culture/music-and-memory/) — how music unlocks recall
- [Rhythm and body: why we want to move](../../culture/rhythm-and-body/) — the body arrives before the ear
- [The problem with the term world music](../../culture/world-music-label/) — **know the frame before you listen**

### Three: hearing it whole — know the structure first

**This layer is most often skipped, and it decides what you are able to hear at all.**

- [The five standard forms](https://theo.midicn.com/) — learn the skeleton of a piece first
- [Music history](https://hist.midicn.com/) and [genres](https://gen.midicn.com/) — background (**both still skeletons**)
- Onsite: [the instrument guide](https://inst.midicn.com/) — what timbre is and where it comes from

## An executable three-step method

If you only have half an hour and no idea where to start:

1. **Listen to a whole piece once, without analysing anything** (this step is most often skipped, and it decides whether any later analysis is enjoyable)
2. **Look up its formal structure** (the form chapters offsite, on the theory site)
3. **Go back and listen again with that structure in mind**, focusing on one thing only (for instance: "what key is the second section in?")

**Step 1 cannot be skipped.** Analysing the score without ever hearing the piece means you are analysing notation, not music.

## What is not built yet

Four honest notes:

1. **There is no listening repertoire** — this site has plenty of general-knowledge and theory, but **no "list of pieces ordered by difficulty"**. That is the biggest gap on this route.
2. **The history and genre sites are skeletons** — so the "background knowledge" layer is effectively only scattered offsite articles.
3. **The tools have no dictation** — all eighteen are "hear it"; none covers "write it down".
4. **The glossary is not enough to look terms up** — and looking terms up is the most frequent action at this layer.

**Point 1 is a genuine gap**: it needs a cross-site ordering of pieces, which is **new content**, not a new structure.
