1877 年之前，「音乐」只能存在于**它正在响起的那一刻**。在那之后，声音第一次可以被装进一个物件、卖出去、运到远处——**今天的整个唱片工业，都是那一次「装罐」的后代。**

## 蜡筒与圆盘：谁先来、谁胜出

爱迪生 1877 年的留声机用锡箔与蜡筒刻录声音；十几年后，柏林纳的「圆盘」唱片登场。**圆筒更早，圆盘胜出**——原因朴素到残酷：圆盘可以平放、堆叠、批量压制。

接下来几十年，产业围绕 78 转唱片组织起来：一首曲子只能装三四分钟，**「三分钟」因此塑造了早期流行歌的长度**。

## 黑胶与「专辑」的诞生

1948 年，密纹唱片（LP）把单面容量拉到二十多分钟——**「专辑」（album）作为一种作品单位，是媒介催生的概念**。此后是盒带（可录、便携）、CD（数字、耐久）。

每一次媒介更替都做同一件事：**重排产业**——谁掌握生产、谁负责发行、听众按什么单位买音乐。

## 数字：从「买物件」到「租访问」

MP3 与随身听把音乐变成文件；流媒体把音乐变成「访问」。这个转向最深的一刀砍在**所有权**上：听众不再拥有一张唱片，只拥有**听的资格**——产业的收入结构（和音乐人的账本）随之彻底改写。

## 一条反复出现的规律

回顾一百五十年，有一条规律几乎从未缺席：

> **媒介换代 → 旧玩家失势、新玩家入场 → 关于「盗版」的大争吵 → 新分配制度落地。**

盒带曾被称为「家庭录音危机」，MP3 时代的争吵更响——而每一次，产业最终都把新媒介吸收成了新收入项。理解这条规律，就理解了为什么今天的争论听起来如此耳熟。

## 听一听：媒体会变，这些音不变

从蜡筒到流媒体，媒介一直在换——但音本身始终是这些音。听一段最朴素的音阶：**一百五十年来，它被刻在蜡上、压进黑胶、写进文件、流进耳机。**

Before 1877, music could only exist **in the moment it sounded**. After that year, sound could be sealed into an object, sold, and shipped far away — **today's entire record industry descends from that first act of canning.**

## Wax and disc: who came first, who won

Edison's 1877 phonograph cut sound into tinfoil and wax cylinders; a decade later Berliner's discs arrived. **The cylinder was first; the disc won** — for reasons almost brutally practical: discs stack, store and press in bulk.

For decades the industry organised itself around 78 rpm records: a side held only three or four minutes, **and "three minutes" accordingly shaped the length of the early popular song**.

## Vinyl and the birth of the "album"

In 1948 the LP stretched a side past twenty minutes — **the "album" as a unit of work is a child of the medium**. Then came cassettes (recordable, portable) and CDs (digital, durable).

Every medium swap did the same one thing: **it rearranged the industry** — who manufactures, who distributes, and in what unit listeners buy music.

## Digital: from buying objects to renting access

MP3 players turned music into files; streaming turned it into "access". The deepest cut was to **ownership**: listeners stopped owning a record and started owning the right to listen — and the industry's revenue structure (and every musician's ledger) was rewritten.

## A rule that keeps returning

Across 150 years, one pattern almost never misses:

> **A new medium → old players stumble, new players enter → a loud argument about "piracy" → a new distribution settlement.**

Cassettes were once called a "home-taping crisis"; the MP3 years were louder — and each time, the industry eventually absorbed the new medium as a new revenue line. Understand the pattern, and today's arguments will sound familiar.

## Listen: media change, these notes do not

From wax to streams, the media kept changing — the notes themselves never did. Hear the plainest scale: **for 150 years it has been cut in wax, pressed in vinyl, written into files, streamed into ears.**
