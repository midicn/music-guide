「我听得出这个音，但写不出谱」——**这句话就是视唱练耳真正的难点所在。** 大多数人卡在中间那一步。

## 三个层次，只有一个是瓶颈

听辨能力其实分三层：

1. **听得出** —— 你能判断这两个音一样/不一样，高/低；
2. **听得出是什么** —— 你能说出它是大二度还是纯五度；
3. **心里能唱出来** —— 你能在没有声音的情况下，在脑内重建它。

绝大多数人卡在第 2 与第 3 之间。他们能辨认音程，**但不能在心里"播放"它**。

第 3 层是瓶颈，因为**它才是通往写谱和演奏的通道**——你在读谱时做的正是这件事：把符号在心里变成声音。

## 为什么"唱"比"认"更有效

一个反直觉的现象：**唱得出的人，比认得出的人强得多。**

原因在于辨认可以靠排除法，唱歌必须精确：

- 听到一个音程，你可以说"比大三度小、比纯四度大"——**近似就够用了**；
- 但要唱出来，你必须知道**它落在哪两个音上**。

所以练耳的经典做法是**先唱后写**：

- 唱 → 唱准了 → 写下 → 对比谱子；
- 而不是：听 → 猜名字 → 写。

**顺序反过来，效率差很多。**

## 三类训练，各自练什么

| 训练 | 练的是 | 常见练法 |
|---|---|---|
| **音程听辨** | 相对音高关系 | 唱旋律的两个音，判断距离 |
| **视唱** | 在脑内重建旋律 | 看着谱直接唱出来，不先分析 |
| **节奏听辨** | 时值与拍点 | 打拍子，念节奏型 |

其中**视唱最关键**，因为它是唯一直接训练"脑内播放"的那一项。

一个常见错误是：**把视唱练成"先分析再唱"**。分析是必要的（这构成第 2 层），但如果每次都从分析开始，**第 3 层永远得不到单独训练**。

## 成年人练耳的三个可行方法

按见效速度排：

1. **哼旋律** —— 拿一段熟悉的歌，脱离伴奏哼出来，再对谱。这是最快的进步方式；
2. **调唱名** —— 用首调唱名唱旋律（唱名见[[concept:melody-ear-training|旋律的听辨]]），因为它不依赖固定音高；
3. **模唱** —— 听一个短乐句，立刻用同样节奏唱出来。**同时训练音高与时值。**

三者都不需要乐器，且可以每天十分钟。

## 一个诚实的说明

练耳的进展**不是线性的**。常见轨迹是：初期进步快 → 平台期 → 突破。平台期通常发生在"能听出但唱不准"的阶段。

**这不是天赋问题，是训练目标切换。** 过了这一关，后面的进展会重新变快。

"I can hear it, but I can't write it down" — **that sentence is the real difficulty of ear training.** Most people are stuck in the middle step.

## Three layers, one bottleneck

Aural ability has three layers:

1. **Hearing that something is there** — same or different, higher or lower;
2. **Hearing what it is** — a major second or a perfect fifth;
3. **Hearing it internally** — reconstructing it in your head with no sound present.

Most people stall between layers 2 and 3. They can identify intervals but **cannot "play" them internally**.

Layer 3 is the bottleneck, because **it is the channel to both notation and playing** — when you read a score you are doing exactly this: turning symbols into sound in your head.

## Why singing beats naming

One counter-intuitive fact: **people who can sing an interval are far stronger than people who can only name it.**

The reason is that naming can use elimination, while singing must be exact:

- Hearing an interval, you can say "bigger than a major third, smaller than a perfect fourth" — **approximation suffices**;
- To sing it, you must know **which two pitches it lands on**.

So the classic order is **sing first, write second**:

- sing → get it right → write it down → compare with the score;
- not: listen → guess the name → write.

**Reversing the order costs a great deal of efficiency.**

## Three kinds of training, three different targets

| Training | What it builds | Common practice |
|---|---|---|
| **Interval hearing** | relative pitch relations | Sing two notes of a melody, judge the distance |
| **Sight-singing** | rebuilding melody internally | Read and sing at once, without analysing first |
| **Rhythm hearing** | duration and pulse | Tap a beat, speak the rhythm pattern |

Of these, **sight-singing matters most**, because it is the only one that trains internal playback directly.

A common mistake: **turning sight-singing into "analyse, then sing"**. Analysis is useful (it builds layer 2), but if you always start from analysis, **layer 3 never gets trained on its own**.

## Three workable methods for adults

Roughly in order of speed of progress:

1. **Hum melodies** — take a song you know, hum it without accompaniment, then check the score. This is the fastest route;
2. **Sing solfège** — sing melodies in movable-do syllables, since it does not depend on fixed pitch;
3. **Echo-sing** — hear a short phrase and immediately sing it back in the same rhythm. **This trains pitch and duration together.**

None needs an instrument, and all fit into ten minutes a day.

## An honest note

Progress in ear training is **not linear**. The usual trajectory: fast early progress → plateau → breakthrough. The plateau sits exactly at "I can hear it but cannot sing it".

**This is not a talent problem; it is a change of training target.** Past it, progress speeds up again.
