一家咖啡馆放背景音乐、一场商场活动播一首歌、一个电台播一段乐曲——这些**公开使用**都不需要"逐案谈判"，但也不能"免费白用"。这中间的制度零件，就是**集体管理组织**。

（制度介绍，不构成法律意见。）

## 为什么必须「集体」

想象一下算术题：全国有百万家店铺、几千个电台频道、无数场活动；而曲库里是千万级的作品、牵涉全球的版权人。

**一对一授权 = 天文数字的谈判。** 于是行业发明了一个"收费站"式的答案：权利人把自己的**特定权利委托给一个组织**统一管理，使用者**向组织交一笔许可费**，就能合法覆盖海量曲目。

## 中国语境下的主要组织

- **中国音乐著作权协会**：管理**音乐作品的词曲权利人**的相关权利（公开表演、广播、复制等）；
- **中国音像著作权集体管理协会**：管理**录音录像制品**的相关权利（如营业场所播放录音、卡拉 OK 等场景）。

**记忆的钥匙还是那两层**：词曲一层、录音一层，各自有不同的"收费站"（见[一首歌上有几层权利](../copyright-composition/)）。

## 它们收什么钱、怎么花

- **使用者的角度**：交一笔年费或项目费 → 拿到**许可** → 合法播放一大片曲库；
- **权利人的角度**：注册成为会员 → 组织替自己收账 → 按**分配规则**领钱；
- **国际网络**：各国的集体管理组织互相代表（通过国际组织网络），所以中国店铺里播外国歌、外国节目里播中国歌，都有相应的流转通道。

## 争议与改进方向

集体管理不是完美的机器——行业里长期的讨论集中在：

- **分配透明度**（钱怎么分、按什么比例、什么时候到账）；
- **覆盖范围**（哪些使用场景还未被纳入）；
- **费率合理性**（对小微使用者的负担）。

**这些争论的存在，恰恰说明它是一套"活的制度"**——在各地的实践中持续调整。

## 给普通人的三个结论

1. **公开使用要交费**——店铺、活动、公播场景都有对应的许可通道；
2. **交费不等于"买下了音乐"**——它买的是特定场景下合法使用的资格；
3. **作为创作者：注册与不注册，是一道商业选择题**——委托管理的代价是执行成本，收益是"有人替你收全球的零钱"。

::: en
A café plays background music; a mall event uses a song; a station broadcasts a piece — these **public uses** need no case-by-case negotiation, yet are not free. The institution that fills this gap is the **collective management organisation**.

(Rules, not legal advice.)

## Why "collective" is unavoidable

Do the arithmetic: a country has millions of shops, thousands of channels, countless events; the catalogue holds tens of millions of works tied to rightsholders worldwide.

**One-to-one licensing means astronomical negotiation.** The industry's answer is a toll-booth design: rightsholders **entrust specific rights to one organisation**; users **pay the organisation a licence fee** and are legally covered across a vast repertoire.

## The main organisations in the Chinese context

- **Music Copyright Society of China (MCSC)**: manages rights of **writers and composers** in musical works (public performance, broadcast, reproduction, etc.);
- **China Audio-Video Copyright Association (CAVCA)**: manages rights in **sound and audiovisual recordings** (in-store playback of recordings, karaoke scenarios, etc.).

**The key to remembering is still the two layers**: work and recording each have their own "toll booth" (see [how many rights live in one song](../copyright-composition/)).

## What they collect and how it flows

- **For users**: pay an annual or project fee → receive a **licence** → play a large repertoire legally;
- **For rightsholders**: register as a member → the organisation collects on your behalf → you are paid under **distribution rules**;
- **International network**: national collectives represent one another, so foreign songs in a Chinese shop and Chinese songs abroad each have a route.

## Debates and directions

Collective management is not a perfect machine — the long-running industry debates centre on:

- **transparency of distribution** (how, in what shares, and when money arrives);
- **coverage** (which uses are still outside);
- **the fairness of tariff levels** (the burden on small users).

**That these debates exist is proof it is a living system** — adjusting through local practice.

## Three conclusions for everyone

1. **Public use requires payment** — shops, events and public playback all have channels;
2. **Paying is not "buying the music"** — it buys a licence for specific uses;
3. **For creators, registering is a business choice** — the cost is administration; the benefit is someone collecting the world's small change for you.
