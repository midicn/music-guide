::: zh
在中文的法律术语里，「著作权」是一把**两股拧成的绳**：一股是**人身权**（和作者的"名"绑定），一股是**财产权**（和"钱"绑定）。它们性格完全不同，所以法律对它们的处理方式也完全不同。

（结构和机制的介绍，不构成法律意见。）

## 人身权：跟着「人」走

中国著作权法给作者的四项人身权：

- **发表权**：决定作品是否公之于众；
- **署名权**：决定以何种方式署名（真名、笔名、不署名）；
- **修改权**：决定作品是否、如何修改；
- **保护作品完整权**：反对歪曲、篡改。

它们的共同点：**通常不能转让、不能继承式"买断"、保护期不受限制**（署名权等的保护不因财产权期满而消失）。

## 财产权：跟着「用」走

财产权是一张**使用方式的清单**：复制、发行、出租、展览、表演、放映、广播、信息网络传播、改编、翻译、汇编……每一项都是"一种可以被使用的动作"。

它们的共同点：**可以转让、可以许可、有保护期限**（期满后作品进入公有领域——见[保护期怎么算](../copyright-duration/)）。

## 一个关键区分：买走的是哪一股

「我把歌全卖给你」在制度上**做不到「全部」**——可以转让的通常是财产权；署名、保护完整这些人身性的部分，**仍然跟着作者**。

这也解释了几件日常的事：

- **署名是义务不是礼貌**：转载、翻唱、改编，署上原作者的名字是人身权的基本要求；
- **「改得好不好」有人管**：恶搞歪曲触碰的是保护作品完整权，与商业利益无关；
- **「买断」也有边界**：合同可以约定很多，但不能把作者从作品里"删掉"。

## 与英美体系的差异

「著作权」（大陆法系传统）天然带着作者中心的人身权色彩；英美传统的「版权」（copyright）最初更偏向"复制权"这个经济工具，后来才逐步补上有限的人身权。

**中国著作权法更接近前者**：人身权四项明文列举，色彩鲜明。所以读英语资料谈 "moral rights" 时要注意——那不是一套东西，是两套传统里的近亲。

::: en
In the civil-law vocabulary, "author's right" is **a rope of two strands**: **moral rights** (bound to the author's name) and **economic rights** (bound to money). Their temperaments differ — so the law handles them differently.

(Structure, not legal advice.)

## Moral rights: they follow the person

Chinese law grants authors four moral rights:

- **Right of disclosure**: deciding whether a work is made public;
- **Right of attribution**: deciding how one's name appears (real name, pen name, none);
- **Right of revision**: deciding whether and how the work is altered;
- **Right of integrity**: objecting to distortion or mutilation.

Their common trait: **generally non-transferable, not subject to buyout, and not limited by protection terms** — attribution does not vanish when economic rights expire.

## Economic rights: they follow use

Economic rights are **a list of uses**: reproduction, distribution, rental, exhibition, performance, screening, broadcast, network communication, adaptation, translation, compilation… each one "an action that can be taken".

Their common trait: **transferable, licensable, and time-limited** (expiry sends the work to the public domain — see [how duration works](../copyright-duration/)).

## A crucial distinction: which strand did you buy?

"I sold you the whole song" cannot mean *everything* under the system: what transfers is usually the economic strand; attribution and integrity **stay with the author**.

This explains daily matters:

- **Attribution is a duty, not politeness**: reposting, covering, adapting — the original name goes on;
- **"How it was changed" matters to someone**: mockery that distorts touches the integrity right, regardless of money;
- **"Buyout" has edges**: a contract can say much, but it cannot delete the author from the work.

## Difference from the common-law world

The civil-law "author's right" is author-centred with a strong moral strand; the common-law "copyright" began more as an economic tool — the right to copy — with limited moral rights added later.

**Chinese law sits closer to the former**: four moral rights, explicitly listed. So when reading English materials on "moral rights", note: not one thing — close cousins from two traditions.
