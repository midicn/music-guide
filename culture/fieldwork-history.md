"把世界上的歌都录下来"——这个念头在十九世纪末到二十世纪初风行过一阵，它塑造了今天很多"传统"的样子。**看一眼采录史的路线，就明白音乐是怎么被保存、又被重新塑造的。**

## 三个阶段，一条主线

### 一 · 声音进入罐子（19 世纪末）
蜡筒录音机让"把声音带回家"成为可能。采录者带着笨重的设备走进社区，录下圣歌、仪式歌、田间调。**这个阶段的天真很动人**：他们真心以为技术能"保存传统"。

问题随即出现：采来的歌，进了博物馆的柜子。

### 二 · 民俗采集运动（20 世纪上半叶）
一批学者意识到"保存"需要系统方法——走访、记录、编目、比较。英国的民俗采集运动最有代表性，产出 massive 的手抄与录音档案。

**贡献**：抢救了大量即将消失的曲目。**局限**：采录者仍是从外部选"代表性"的人，常常漏掉日常最普通的声音。

### 三 · 跨国采录与档案化（20 世纪后半叶）
胶带、数字化、国际合作项目。采录规模空前，档案馆越来越多。**同时争议也空前**：

- 谁来决定"值得录"（见上方实例 3：黑人灵歌的采录史就是一部权力史）；
- 录音脱离使用场景后，意义还剩多少（见[录音如何改变民间音乐](../recording-changes-folk/)）；
- 档案的所有权与回访义务（见[田野录音的伦理](../fieldwork-ethics/)）。

### 四 · 数字与再语境化（21 世纪）
硬盘、手机、云。录音成本趋零。**新的问题随之而来**：一首歌被录下、发到网上之后，谁还能"拥有"它的语境？当全世界都能录音时，采录本身还有意义吗？

## 一条贯穿的主线

**采录史，其实是"保存"这个理想不断被修正的历史。**

从"技术上能录下就等于保存了"（天真）→ "要连场景一起录"（方法论）→ "要问谁的歌、谁的同意"（伦理）→ "要考虑录音之后它会变成什么"（后果）。

**每一步，都是上一代的善意留给下一代的功课。**

## 采录史留给我们的三个判断

1. **录音是档案，也是表演**——按下按钮的人参与了音乐的形成（这也是[[concept:timbre|音色]]研究的新入口）；
2. **"代表性"从来是选择的结果**——没有"全貌"这种东西，只有采录者的眼光；
3. **保存有保质期**——脱离场景的录音，会在几代之内失去它原本的力量（这是数字时代最尖锐的问题）。

"Record all the world's songs" — the idea swept through the late nineteenth and early twentieth century, and it shaped how many "traditions" look today. **Read the route of the recording history, and you see how music is preserved and re-shaped.**

## Three stages, one thread

### One · Sound enters the can (late 19th century)
Wax-cylinder machines made "bringing sound home" possible. Collectors walked into communities with bulky equipment, recording hymns, ritual songs, field tunes. **The naivety of this stage is moving**: they genuinely believed technology could "preserve tradition".

The problem arrived fast: the collected songs went into museum cabinets.

### Two · The folk-song movement (early 20th century)
A generation of scholars realised that preservation needs method — visiting, recording, cataloguing, comparing. The British folk-song movement is the emblem, producing vast manuscript and sound archives.

**Contribution**: rescued huge amounts of repertoire about to vanish. **Limit**: collectors still chose the "representative" from outside, and routinely missed the most ordinary sounds of daily life.

### Three · Transnational collection and the archive (late 20th century)
Tape, digitisation, international projects. Unprecedented scale, unprecedented controversy:

- Who decides what is "worth recording" (example 3 above — the collecting history of spirituals is a history of power);
- Once separated from its use, how much meaning remains (see [how recording changes folk music](../recording-changes-folk/));
- Archive ownership and the duty to return (see [fieldwork ethics](../fieldwork-ethics/)).

### Four · Digital and recontextualised (21st century)
Hard drives, phones, cloud. Recording cost approaches zero — and new questions follow: once a song is recorded and posted, who can still "own" its context? When anyone can record everything, what does fieldwork still mean?

## The thread running through it

**The history of recording is the history of the ideal of "preservation" being corrected, again and again.**

From "if it can be recorded it is preserved" (naive) → "the context must be recorded too" (methodological) → "whose song, whose consent" (ethical) → "consider what it will become afterwards" (consequential).

**At each step, one generation's good intentions become the next generation's homework.**

## Three judgements the history leaves us

1. **A recording is both archive and performance** — the person who presses the button helped shape the music (a new doorway into [[concept:timbre|timbre]] research);
2. **"Representativeness" is always the result of selection** — there is no "whole"; there are collectors' eyes;
3. **Preservation has a shelf life** — a recording detached from its occasion loses its force within generations (the sharpest problem of the digital age).
