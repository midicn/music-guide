成人学琴的第一课通常是：**你不是学生，所以你不用当学生。** 这话看着平常，但它推翻了一整套沿用的教学设计。

## 儿童班和成人班的根本差异

| | 儿童班 | 成人班 |
|---|---|---|
| 时间结构 | 每天固定，连续 | 每周几次，**经常被打断** |
| 目标来源 | 家长定的 | 自己定的 |
| 主要障碍 | 能力不足 | **持续性** |
| 有效单位 | 长时间、集中 | 短时间、可重复 |
| 反馈需求 | 少，需要鼓励 | 多，需要具体 |

**最大的差别是时间结构。** 成年人每天 20 分钟，中间还要被工作打断——**这不是意志问题，是结构问题。**

## 所以成人教材应该长什么样

三个可推导的特征：

1. **短** —— 上面第三个实例那种二十五首渐进短曲，一首两分钟。**能弹完是心理上的关键**；
2. **完整** —— 一首小奏鸣曲而非一段练习。它有开头、有结尾，弹完有"我做完了东西"的感觉；
3. **可以随时放下** —— 上面的第二个实例分成四首而不是一首长的，就是这个道理：**一首长的曲子被打断三次以后，人会本能地不再拿起它。**

上面第一个实例说的就是这个：二十首初级小曲，难度不高、完整、值得弹完——**这三项的优先级高于「能弹什么名曲」**。

## 成人最常见的失败不是难，是「不开始」

一个反直觉的现象：成人班的弃课率高峰不在"觉得太难"，而在**"没时间"**和**"怕被看出来水平低"**。

对应的两个有效设计：

- **允许降难度**：能弹得比学生预想的简单，反而是好事；
- **去掉"被评价"**：成人班最有效的氛围是**没有观众**——不需要给谁弹。

上面第二个实例的"四首短曲"还有第三层作用：**每次上课都能拿出完整的东西**，这是对抗"我什么也没做成"的关键心理装置。

## 一个可执行的成人练习结构

按"可被打断"设计，而不是按"理想状态"设计：

1. **每次只定一个小目标**（不是"练完这首"，是"这段八小节的换指"）；
2. **目标必须在 15 分钟内可完成**；
3. **完成即止**，不追加（因为下一次可能隔三天）；
4. **每次留一个"下次起点"**——写在谱上，下次直接开始。

第 4 条是防止中断的关键：**如果中断后需要重新热身，多数人就放弃了。**

## 一个要直说的事实

成人学习者的劣势常被说成"手型僵、记谱慢、时间少"。这些是真的，但**更值得说的是优势**：

- **你知道自己喜欢什么**——这解决了儿童班最麻烦的问题（动机）；
- **你能读谱**——很多成人的读谱能力其实优于同龄儿童；
- **你不需要别人说你弹得好**——一旦不需要，你的练习量可以大得多。

**"没时间"是真的，但它常被用来解释一个更根本的问题：曲目不是你真正想要的。**

The first lesson for an adult beginner usually goes like this: **you are not a student, so you do not have to be one.** That sounds plain, but it overturns a whole inherited design.

## The real difference between children's and adults' classes

| | Children's class | Adult class |
|---|---|---|
| Time structure | fixed daily, continuous | a few times a week, **constantly interrupted** |
| Where the goal comes from | the parent | the learner |
| Main obstacle | insufficient ability | **continuity** |
| Effective unit | long and concentrated | short and repeatable |
| Feedback need | little, encouragement helps | plenty, and specific |

**The biggest difference is time structure.** Adults get 20 minutes a day with work interruptions. **That is not a willpower problem; it is a structural one.**

## So adult material should look like this

Three features follow directly:

1. **Short** — the progressive études in the third example run about two minutes each. **Finishing is psychologically decisive**;
2. **Complete** — a sonatina rather than an exercise. It has a beginning and an end, and finishing it gives the feeling of having made something;
3. **Put-down-able** — the second example splits into four sonatinas rather than one long piece, for exactly this reason: **after a long piece has been interrupted three times, people instinctively stop picking it up.**

The first example says the same thing: twenty little beginner pieces, not technically hard, complete, and worth finishing — **all three of those rank above "which famous piece can I play".**

## The most common adult failure is not difficulty; it is "not starting"

Counter-intuitively, adult dropout peaks not at "this is too hard" but at **"I have no time"** and **"I am afraid they will see how bad I am."**

Two designs that work against those:

- **Allowing a lower difficulty**: sounding easier than the learner expected is a *good* outcome;
- **Removing the evaluation**: the most effective adult-class atmosphere has **no audience** — nothing to perform for.

The "four short pieces" in the second example carries a third function: **something complete can be brought to every lesson**, which is the key psychological device against "I have achieved nothing".

## An executable adult practice structure

Design for "interruptible", not for the ideal day:

1. **One small target per session** (not "finish the piece", but "that finger change in those eight bars");
2. **The target must be completable in 15 minutes**;
3. **Stop when it is done** — do not top up, because the next session may be three days away;
4. **Leave a "next starting point" written on the score** so the following session begins immediately.

Point 4 is what defeats interruption: **if resuming requires a warm-up, most people give up.**

## One thing worth stating plainly

Adult weaknesses are often listed as "stiff hands, slow reading, no time". All true, but **the advantage is more worth stating**:

- **You know what you like** — which solves the hardest problem in children's classes (motivation);
- **You can already read music** — many adults read better than children of the same age;
- **You no longer need anyone to say you played well** — once you don't, your practice hours can be far higher.

**"No time" is real, but it is often used to explain a more fundamental problem: the repertoire is not what you actually want.**
