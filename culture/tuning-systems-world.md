钢琴有 88 个键，黑键排成一排——于是大多数人以为"音就是这么排的"。**其实十二平均律只是众多选择中的一个，而且是很年轻的一个。**

## 十二平均律的胜利

十二平均律的核心主张非常简洁：**所有半音等宽**，于是任何调都能自由转调，管弦乐与和声学才得以在十九世纪定型。

它今天的地位来自**实际的胜利**：钢琴普及、键盘乐器成为标准、转调自由成了作曲的基本假设。

## 但"标准"其实很年轻

一个被忽略的事实：**等律制在西方普及是十九世纪的事。**

在那之前，用纯律（让三度、五度尽可能"纯"）、中音律等方案的音乐，泛音更"干净"，但转调困难。巴赫时代的"平均律钢琴"其实并不是完全等律——真正的等律钢琴是之后才被调音师推广开的。

**换句话说：你从小听的那个"标准"，是一个工业与审美共同选出来的方案。**

## 其他体系

- **五声与调式音阶**：世界多数地区的基础组织（见[[concept:major-scale|大调音阶]]一节里的讨论）；
- **"所罗门音阶"家族**：教会调式的一个分支，常用非十二平均的音高（见实例 1）；
- **微分音实践**：一些传统用比半音更小的音高单位，让特定的旋律语汇成立；
- **格鲁吉亚、中东、东亚等系统**：各有自己的音阶组合与伴奏逻辑。

**它们不是"不精确的十二平均律"，而是不同的问题用了不同解法。**

## 一个需要说清的区分

- **音律**（tuning）：具体音高怎么定——同一首曲子的"标准音高"可以差几十音分；
- **音阶/调式**（scale/mode）：从这些音里怎么组织旋律。

**世界音乐的多样性主要在后者，但真正让人"听起来别扭"的往往是前者**——这也是为什么"同一个调，不同的琴听起来不同"。

## 实践含义

- **听世界音乐时**，先接受"调高与音程宽度可能不同"，再评判旋律；
- **读谱时**，若用钢琴谱记非十二平均的音高，**升降号会变得不精确**——这是记谱法的局限，不是音乐的错误；
- **在键盘上弹世界音乐**：钢琴可以弹绝大多数旋律，但**伴奏的音高习惯会带着"西方耳朵"走**（见[口传与书面](../oral-vs-written/)）。

The piano has 88 keys, the black ones in neat rows — so most people assume pitches are simply "arranged that way". **Twelve-tone equal temperament is one choice among many, and a young one.**

## The victory of equal temperament

Its central claim is simple: **all semitones are equal**, so any key can modulate freely — and orchestral tonality could settle in the nineteenth century.

Its status today comes from **practical victory**: the piano spread, keyboards became standard, free modulation became a basic assumption of composition.

## But the "standard" is young

An overlooked fact: **equal temperament spread in the West only in the nineteenth century.**

Before that, just intonation (pure thirds and fifths as far as possible) and other schemes gave purer overtones but made modulation hard. Bach's "well-tempered" pianos were not truly equal-tempered — true equal tuning was promoted later by piano tuners.

**In other words: the "standard" you grew up with is a scheme chosen jointly by industry and aesthetics.**

## Other systems

- **Pentatonic and modal scales**: the base organisation across much of the world (see [[concept:major-scale|Major Scale]]);
- **The so-called "Solomonic" family**: a branch of church modes using non-equal pitches (example 1);
- **Microtonal practice**: some traditions use units smaller than a semitone so that particular melodic vocabularies work;
- **Georgian, Middle Eastern, East Asian systems**: each with its own scale combinations and accompaniment logic.

**They are not "impure equal temperament" but different answers to different questions.**

## A distinction worth making

- **Tuning**: exactly which pitches — the same piece's "standard pitch" can differ by tens of cents;
- **Scale/mode**: how melodies are organised from those pitches.

**The world's musical diversity lives mostly in the second, yet what sounds "off" to outsiders is often the first** — which is why the same key sounds different on different instruments.

## Practical implications

- **Listening to world music**: accept that tuning and interval widths may differ before judging the melody;
- **Reading scores**: when non-equal pitches are notated for piano, **accidentals become imprecise** — a limit of notation, not an error in the music;
- **Playing world music on a keyboard**: the piano can play most melodies, but **accompaniment habits carry "Western ears" along** (see [oral and written](../oral-vs-written/)).
