::: zh
音乐史不是一张年份表。它更像一部侦探剧——**每个时代都有自己的「口音」，听得出差别，年份自己会各就各位。**

## 抓手一：声音指纹

先只做一件事：给每个时代听 1 分钟，描述「它听起来像什么」。

| 时代 | 声音指纹（怎么听） |
|---|---|
| 中世纪 | 单声、空旷、像在石头大厅里回响 |
| 文艺复兴 | 人声贴合、几个声部平等交织，找不到「主角」 |
| 巴洛克 | 持续的律动 + 精细的装饰，情绪统一 |
| 古典 | 清晰、对称、乐句像句子一样有标点 |
| 浪漫 | 个人的起伏放大，速度与力度都更自由 |
| 二十世纪以后 | 主动打破规则——从「好听」转向「表达」 |

提示：**每格先找到一首代表作听熟**，再记这一格的年份。

## 抓手二：技术节点

历史的大转弯，往往由**技术**推动——记住这几件事，比背年代有用：

- **记谱法**的成熟 → 音乐可以被精确复制，跨代传播；
- **复调**的发明 → 多个声部同时说话，欧洲音乐的独特路线由此展开；
- **印刷术与乐谱出版** → 音乐成为商品，业余爱好者变多；
- **平均律**的普及 → 可以自由转调，和声空间打开；
- **录音**的发明 → 音乐第一次可以被「保存」，二十世纪之后的一切都变了。

## 抓手三：中国音乐史是并行的主线

中国有一条**独立发展、同样长**的音乐史：八音分类、十二律、戏曲、民乐。——不要把它「插入」西方的时间线，而是当作**另一条主线**并排看：两者解决的问题相似（音怎么定、曲怎么传），答案各异。

## 听一听：纯五度——最早的空旷感

中世纪和文艺复兴的复调，常用「纯五度」这种最干净的音程搭建——它没有色彩倾向，听起来空而稳，正是那些石头大厅的气质。

::: en
Music history is not a table of dates. It is a detective series — **every era has its own accent, and once you can hear the difference, the dates find their places on their own.**

## Handle one: the earprint

Do one thing first: give each era a minute, and describe "what it sounds like".

| Era | Earprint (what to listen for) |
|---|---|
| Medieval | Single line, open, echoing in a stone hall |
| Renaissance | Voices woven as equals — no one leading |
| Baroque | A steady pulse + fine ornament — one mood throughout |
| Classical | Clear, symmetric — phrases with punctuation |
| Romantic | Amplified personal feeling; tempo and dynamics set free |
| The twentieth century onward | Rules broken on purpose — from "pretty" to "expressive" |

Tip: **learn one representative piece per square first**, then file the dates.

## Handle two: technology's turns

History's sharp turns usually come from **technology**. Remember a few of these instead of memorising decades:

- a mature **notation** → music can be copied exactly and travel across generations;
- the invention of **polyphony** → several voices at once; Europe's distinctive path begins;
- **printing and music publishing** → music becomes a commodity; amateurs multiply;
- the spread of **equal temperament** → keys change freely; harmonic space opens up;
- the invention of **recording** → music becomes storable, and everything after is different.

## Handle three: China runs a parallel main line

China has a music history just as long, **developed independently**: the eight-instrument categories, the twelve-lü pitch system, opera, instrumental traditions. Do not insert it into the Western timeline — read it as **another main line beside it**. The questions were similar (how to fix pitches, how to pass pieces on); the answers differed.

## Listen: the perfect fifth, the earliest emptiness

Medieval and Renaissance polyphony often built on the perfect fifth — the cleanest interval, with no colouring, sounding open and steady, exactly the mood of those stone halls.
